# Proven Micro-SaaS Patterns and Evidence-Backed B2B Ideas for a Tiny Bootstrapped Team (late 2026)

> Research method note: all evidence below came from web-search result summaries (Nov 2026). Direct page fetches were blocked in this session for several domains (indiehackers.com, bigideasdb.com), so figures could not be checked against full primary pages. Many "micro-SaaS statistics" pages online are SEO/vendor content with conflicting numbers; these are flagged. MicroConf's latest State of Independent SaaS report I could find is the 2024 edition (data collected late 2023) — older data, marked as such.

## 1. Which small B2B SaaS products publicly report MRR, and what patterns do they share?

### Takeaway
Publicly verified MRR exists mainly on TrustMRR (Stripe-verified) and in founder interviews (Starter Story, Indie Hackers). The repeat winners for tiny teams are "boring" single-job tools (backups, cron monitoring, geocoding, screenshots, data connectors), plugins/apps inside larger platforms, and hidden vertical niches where the buyer is easy to locate but invisible to mainstream founders. Most products, however, earn very little: the typical verified micro-SaaS is well under $1k MRR.

### Cited Findings
**Distribution of outcomes (expect low median):**
- BigIdeasDB's analysis of TrustMRR data: median MRR across 3,700+ revenue-reporting startups was $145 (Sept 2026 re-measurement), 75th percentile $894, mean $4,298 (~30x median) — [BigIdeasDB SaaS Metrics Benchmarks 2026](https://bigideasdb.com/saas-metrics-benchmarks-2026); the same site elsewhere claims a median of ~$7 MRR across 3,478 revenue-positive startups — [BigIdeasDB TrustMRR overview](https://bigideasdb.com/docs/trustmrr-overview). These conflict; treat as directional (median is tiny).
- A 2026 benchmark article: ~70% of revenue-generating micro-SaaS earn under $1,000 MRR; only 6.1% clear $10,000 MRR (methodology unverified) — [mean.ceo Micro-SaaS Revenue Statistics 2026](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11213).
- Indie Hackers: median active indie hackers make under $1K/month; top 10% make $10K+ MRR — [Indie Hackers post](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-bootstrapped-my-software-business-to-1-000-mrr-3239bfb707).
- MicroConf (2024 report, data from late 2023 — older): 65% of companies have just 1–10 paying customers; companies with full-time founders grow ~30% faster than part-time — [MicroConf 0–10k ARR page](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr), [MicroConf State of Independent SaaS 2024](https://microconf.com/saas-this-year).
- A blog citing Freemius (2025): median profitable micro-SaaS ~ $4.2K MRR with 64–76% margins (secondary, unverified) — [mean.ceo Micro-SaaS Business Statistics](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11171).
- TrustMRR tracks 6,040+ verified startups across 31 categories, >$30M aggregate monthly revenue (aggregator figure) — [BigIdeasDB TrustMRR overview](https://bigideasdb.com/docs/trustmrr-overview); category averages: Content Creation highest at ~$9,000 average MRR across 400+ startups — [BigIdeasDB TrustMRR](https://bigideasdb.com/trustmrr).

**Named small products with reported revenue (and their niche):**
- Healthchecks.io (cron/uptime monitoring, one part-time developer): 652 paying customers, $14,043 MRR in July 2024 — [MicroGaps blog](https://www.microgaps.com/blog/boring-micro-saas-ideas-that-make-money) (devtools — overlaps another researcher).
- SimpleBackups (automated database/file backups): validated $16K MRR — [MicroGaps blog](https://www.microgaps.com/blog/boring-micro-saas-ideas-that-make-money).
- Geocodio (address → coordinates API, husband-and-wife team): crossed $1M all-time revenue by 2019 (old data) — [MicroGaps blog](https://www.microgaps.com/blog/boring-micro-saas-ideas-that-make-money).
- ScreenshotOne (screenshot API, solo): $12K/month MRR with 280 customers — [Starter Story via Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-built-it-12k-month-micro-saas?t=0).
- Data Fetcher (Airtable data-import plugin, a platform add-on): ~$23,000/month recurring — [Starter Story via Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-built-it-23k-month-micro-saas?t=0).
- BlockToPin (Pinterest automation): $16K+ MRR by being "better, faster, cheaper" in a narrow niche — [Starter Story summary](https://bulletpointpodcast.pages.dev/starterstory/how-i-built-it-16k-month-micro-saas).
- TrustMRR verified (Oct 8, 2026): Postiz $251,282 MRR, POST BRIDGE $48,915, Web3Forms $41,789, DataFast $30,729 (general-purpose tools, mostly marketer/dev audiences) — [MicroGaps blog](https://www.microgaps.com/blog/boring-micro-saas-ideas-that-make-money); TrustMRR itself ~ $31k revenue/30 days — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/trustmrr).
- Indie Hackers directory lists Buttondown at $83K+ MRR and TalkThrive at $11K MRR — [Indie Hackers stories](https://www.indiehackers.com/stories).
- Parakeet Chat (hidden niche: AI messaging/learning app for US federal prisoners, families pay $15–20/month): founder says a little over $300K revenue in 2025, ~$1.5M lifetime; third-party profile ~ $25–30K MRR, ~1,800 subs, $1.2M asking price; no UI/app store presence, almost no indexed competitors — [Superframeworks case study](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/parakeet-chat), [Starter Story via Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/i-made-1-5m-from-an-app-you-ve-never-heard-of). (B2C payer, but a strong "hidden niche" pattern.)
- Funeral-home tool built on Airtable + Zapier, launched through funeral home association newsletters: $29k MRR in 12 months, 45 locations at $650/month — single Medium anecdote, unverified and partly projected — [Medium: Vertical SaaS micro-niches](https://medium.com/@urano10/vertical-saas-micro-niches-beyond-the-obvious-01e0adf16a98).
- A bootstrapped portfolio including a digital-signage platform for cafes, gyms, schools and retail: just over $200,000 MRR combined — [Medium: Vertical SaaS micro-niches](https://medium.com/@urano10/vertical-saas-micro-niches-beyond-the-obvious-01e0adf16a98).
- Acquire.com listing example: a bundle of 3 SaaS apps + 1 Slack app + 2 Monday.com apps at ~ $750 MRR ($9k ARR) — [Superframeworks: places to sell micro-SaaS](https://superframeworks.com/articles/best-places-sell-startup-microsaas) (platform-app pattern; small but real).

**Exits / marketplaces:**
- Microns.io targets micro-SaaS under $100K; Acquire.com is the largest marketplace (2,200+ active listings, 55.7% SaaS); median Acquire.com multiple across 2024–2025 ~3.9x annual profit; AI businesses rose from 4.6% to 10.5% of Acquire.com submissions. Fee figures conflict between sources — [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/articles/best-places-sell-startup-microsaas), [Vaulto](https://vaulto.sh/blog/best-saas-marketplaces-2026), [startupa.ge](https://startupa.ge/blog/best-startup-marketplaces-buy-sell-saas).

### Inferences
- Pattern 1 — "single painful job, priced per business": backups, monitoring, screenshots, geocoding. Many are dev-facing (other researchers cover devtools), but the principle transfers to vertical ops: one job, done reliably, that the buyer would otherwise do by hand.
- Pattern 2 — "platform plugin": Data Fetcher (Airtable) and the Slack/Monday app bundle show that distribution via a platform marketplace can produce revenue without SEO or ads, but ARPU and ceiling depend on the host platform.
- Pattern 3 — "hidden niche with a reachable buyer": Parakeet (prison families) and the funeral-home anecdote both rely on a closed community channel (trade newsletters, prison word-of-mouth) where competitors are invisible. This is the pattern most compatible with a non-technical salesperson doing outreach.
- Pattern 4 — "high price per location in a vertical": $650/location (funeral) vs $15/month (tutor tools) shows vertical B2B ARPU varies by 40x; the team should prefer verticals where a single customer already pays $100+/month for software.
- The median outcome is near zero, so idea choice should optimize for fast, cheap validation with pre-sales rather than for TAM.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve TrustMRR category-level medians for B2B verticals (bigideasdb.com blocked, TrustMRR category pages not in search results).
- Found no publicly reported MRR for small association, church, sports-club or driving-school software founders; this was searched and nothing came back.
- Could not confirm whether a MicroConf 2025 State of Independent SaaS report exists; Freemius' 2025 report could only be read via excerpts.

## 2. Which verticals are under-digitized but have budgets?

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is thin, but the signals that exist point to: nonprofits/grant seekers (an established paid tool category with $50–$179/month price points), public-tender bidders (existing AI RFP tools price at $500–$900/month for teams, leaving a small-business gap), tutoring/education businesses (low ARPU, crowded by cheap tools), funeral homes (high ARPU anecdote), and multi-site local businesses (signage, gyms). SMB AI adoption is rising but uneven, which favors tools that bundle AI inside a familiar workflow rather than selling "AI".

### Cited Findings
- Grant software is an established paid category: Instrumentl plans start at $179/mo; Grantable paid plans start at $50/mo with free funder discovery (pricing as of May 2026); Grantable claims 30,000+ grant professionals — [Grantable AI grant writer page](https://grantable.co/lp/ai-grant-writer); a third-party review lists Grantable at free / $50 / $150 per month with a 50% small-nonprofit discount (unverified) — [aitoolsbakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/grantable-pricing/). Many small entrants on Capterra (Imagagrant, ScriptaGrant, PureGrant) — [Capterra Imagagrant](https://www.capterra.com/p/10036878/Imagagrant/), [Capterra ScriptaGrant](https://www.capterra.com/p/10049361/ScriptaGrant/).
- AI RFP response tools: AutoRFP.ai does not publish pricing; estimated $500–$800/month for small teams — [SiftHub on AutoRFP](https://www.sifthub.io/blog/autorfp-pricing-reviews-features-breakdown); competitor Inventive lists AutoRFP at $899/mo billed yearly — [Inventive on AutoRFP](https://www.inventive.ai/blog-posts/autorfp-ai-pricing-features-reviews); DeepRFP targets proposal consultants and small businesses — [Capterra DeepRFP](https://www.capterra.com/p/10028620/DeepRFP/); Proposify starts at $29/month (proposal tool, not RFP-specific) — [Inventive on Proposify](https://www.inventive.ai/blog-posts/proposify-pricing-features-reviews).
- Tutoring software is cheap and crowded: TutorBird $14.95/month + $4.95 per additional tutor, 30-day trial — [TutorBird](https://www.tutorbird.com/?p=75253); Teachworks example plan $16.49 + $0.32 per lesson (competitor's blog) — [Teach n Go](https://rv.teachngo.com/blog/teachworks-vs-tutorbird); Teachworks also listed under driving-school software — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/driving-school-software/a/teachworks).
- Gym/studio vertical has funded incumbents such as PushPress (boutique gyms, CrossFit, yoga, martial arts) — [SaaSClub / search summary](https://saasclub.io/podcast/product-market-fit/).
- Funeral-home vertical anecdote: $650/location/month, sold through association newsletters (unverified) — [Medium](https://medium.com/@urano10/vertical-saas-micro-niches-beyond-the-obvious-01e0adf16a98).
- Expiration/licence/certification tracking for small businesses is repeatedly cited as an unsexy niche (estimated $7,000–$18,000 MRR potential; listicle estimate, not reported revenue) — [Redwerk](https://redwerk.com/blog/micro-saas-ideas-that-print-money/), [MicroGaps](https://www.microgaps.com/blog/boring-micro-saas-ideas-that-make-money).
- SMB AI adoption (2026) varies from ~17% to 76% depending on survey wording: Goldman Sachs (~1,256 owners, early 2026) ~76% use AI in some form; Fed Small Business Credit Survey 46% of employer firms use AI, +15% planning; Census Bureau broadened question 17–20% by May 2026; NFIB 24%; Thryv (561 SMBs, Apr 2026) 66%, up from 55% — [search summary of multiple surveys incl. US Chamber 2026 report](https://www.uschamber.com/assets/documents/Empowering-Small-Business-The-Impact-of-Technology-on-U.S.-Small-Business-Small-Business-Report-2026.pdf), [CPA Practice Advisor, Jul 2026: 70% say they need more training](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/07/14/ai-adoption-continues-to-rise-but-70-say-they-need-more-training/186565/), [Fortune, Mar 2026: slow integration across operations](https://dc.fortune.com/2026/03/18/small-business-ai-slow-integration-across-operations).
- 70% of business owners are paying for AI tools (fully paid or mixed free/paid) — [BizBuySell 2026](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/small-business-ai-adoption-2026/).
- Vertical/micro-SaaS platforms reportedly achieve 35–60% higher retention than horizontal tools (Skywinds report via a newsletter; secondary) — [search summary citing Freemius context](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/); one article claims vertical niches grow 2–3x faster than horizontal (secondary) — [Stormy blog](https://stormy.ai/blog/find-profitable-micro-saas-ideas-niche-down-strategy).

### Inferences
- Best-evidenced budgeted verticals for this team: (1) grant seekers and grant consultants, (2) small suppliers bidding on public tenders, (3) membership organizations/associations, (4) funeral homes, (5) multi-location clinics/care providers needing credential tracking. Tutoring and gyms have budgets but are price-anchored low or held by funded incumbents.
- "SMBs need help with AI" (70% want training) suggests packaging AI as an invisible feature in a workflow tool (drafts, summaries, reminders) rather than as an AI product.
- EU public procurement data (TED) and UK/US tender portals are public and free, which suits a developer with AI skills and a Sweden-based English salesperson.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–2026 data found on software spend per vertical for associations, churches, sports clubs, driving schools, vet clinics or translation agencies.
- Competitor pricing for association management (e.g., Wild Apricot), volunteer scheduling, funeral-home software and driving-school software was not retrieved; must be verified during validation.

## 3. What do 2025–2026 data and founder retrospectives say about time to first $1k MRR, pricing, churn, channels, and AI-wrapper failure?

### Takeaway
There is no rigorous dataset on time to first $1k MRR; playbooks cite 8–12 weeks after launch as a target, while real anecdotes range from weeks to a full year. Hybrid/usage pricing is reported to beat flat subscriptions, narrow-market cold outreach is the most reliable first-customer channel, and AI wrappers fail on retention (thin product, vendor absorption, no proprietary data/workflow).

### Cited Findings
**Time to revenue**
- Playbooks put $1K MRR at ~8–12 weeks; another benchmark table puts $200–$1K MRR at months 3–6 (targets, not measured outcomes) — [DesignRevision playbook](https://designrevision.com/blog/how-to-start-a-saas-business), [MRRStory guide](https://www.mrrstory.com/blog/saas-0-to-10k-mrr-guide), [EarlyCustomers founder playbook](https://earlycustomers.com/resources/founder-playbook/).
- Anecdotes: one founder ended year one at ~ $1,000 MRR; another got first $1K by cold-DMing Shopify store owners complaining on X — [DEV: Zero to $1k MRR](https://dev.to/daimzia/from-zero-to-1k-mrr-a-practical-saas-playbook-for-solo-developers-44f3).
- Outliers exist (Rob Hoffman claims $20K MRR in 30 days and $61K MRR in 53 days for two SaaS) — [X post](https://x.com/RobHoffman_/status/1998189948437221838) — survivorship bias, not representative.
- MicroConf (2024, older): full-time founders grow ~30% faster than part-time — [MicroConf](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr). Relevant: this founder has ~15 h/week.

**Pricing**
- Freemius 2025: usage-based and hybrid models outperformed flat subscriptions; AI-using products monetize at a higher rate (61% vs 54%, secondary citation); ~50% of independent SaaS run solo (secondary) — [Freemius State of Micro-SaaS 2025](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/).
- MicroConf 2024 report covered credit-card-upfront trials and growth by target market (details not retrieved) — [Startups for the Rest of Us ep. 721](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-721-7-key-takeaways-from-the-2024-state-of-independent-saas-report).
- Flat pricing across segments used deliberately by a bootstrapped vertical SaaS to avoid building multiple products — [SaaSClub search summary](https://saasclub.io/podcast/product-market-fit/).
- Starter Story niche-app founder: monetization should respond to user behavior rather than start as a guess — [Starter Story summary](https://bulletpointpodcast.pages.dev/starterstory/i-built-a-niche-app-to-9k-mrr).

**Churn**
- AI-wrapper churn claims: average 90-day churn ~65% vs ~35% SaaS average; median 30-day retention 23% vs 45% for traditional SaaS (blog figures, unverified) — [AI Product Weekly, Mar 2026](https://aiproductweekly.blogspot.com/2026/03/why-90-of-ai-wrapper-products-will-fail.html), [HatchWorks](https://hatchworks.com/blog/gen-ai/ai-wrapper-product-strategy/).
- Vertical micro-SaaS reportedly 35–60% higher retention than horizontal tools (secondary) — see Section 2.
- Gap: no trustworthy 2025–2026 churn benchmark for sub-$10k-MRR B2B SaaS found.

**Channels**
- Cold email: one playbook suggests ~500 personalized emails to reach ~10 paying customers in months 1–3, assuming 3–5% reply rates (assumption, not measured); cold outreach works best when the market is narrow and reachable — [DesignRevision](https://designrevision.com/blog/how-to-start-a-saas-business), [Micro SaaS Idea newsletter #44](https://microsaasidea.substack.com/p/micro-saas-products-around-cold-outreach).
- A Starter Story analysis found social media, blogging and building in public were largely wastes of time for one micro-SaaS — [Stormy summary](https://stormy.ai/blog/micro-saas-one-feature-playbook).
- MicroConf/Freemius survey: among founders who run ads, 57% ... (finding truncated in sources) — [Freemius](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/).
- Trade-association newsletters as launch channel (funeral anecdote) — [Medium](https://medium.com/@urano10/vertical-saas-micro-niches-beyond-the-obvious-01e0adf16a98); platform marketplaces (Airtable, Slack, Monday.com) as distribution — [Starter Story Data Fetcher](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-built-it-23k-month-micro-saas?t=0), [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/articles/best-places-sell-startup-microsaas).

**Why AI-wrapper startups fail (2026 commentary, mostly opinion blogs)**
- Model vendors absorb features (claim: OpenAI releases cannibalized 200+ funded GPT-wrapper startups in 2024), inference eats margins, nothing stops copycats, low switching costs among AI-savvy users — [Tian Pan, Apr 2026](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/12/the-ai-wrapper-trap-when-your-moat-is-someone-elses-api-call), [eljo blog, Feb 2026](https://eljo-bgx.pages.dev/blog/2026-02-09-ai-wrappers-startup-trap), [Alex Cloudstar](https://www.alexcloudstar.com/blog/ai-wrappers-dead-what-to-build-instead-2026/).
- "Thin" wrappers lose the majority of users within 90 days; survivors have workflow depth, proprietary data, integrations — [HatchWorks](https://hatchworks.com/blog/gen-ai/ai-wrapper-product-strategy/), [AI Product Weekly](https://aiproductweekly.blogspot.com/2026/03/why-90-of-ai-wrapper-products-will-fail.html).
- Failure-rate claims (80–90% by end of 2026, attributed to CB Insights/Gartner) could not be confirmed — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/autonainews/90-of-ai-wrapper-startups-face-2026-closures-as-value-add-strategy-crumbles-7d4).

### Inferences
- Realistic plan for this team: with ~15 h/week and an 8-week MVP, first paying customer is plausible around weeks 8–14 only if pre-sales/design partners are lined up during build; $1k MRR more likely in months 4–9. Treat 8–12-week playbook figures as optimistic.
- Pricing guidance: B2B vertical tools should start at $49–$149/month per organization (not $9–$19), with a usage component (per seat, per location, per tender/grant/bid) once value is proven; offer annual pre-pay to design partners to fund the €800 budget.
- Channel guidance for a non-technical English-speaking salesperson: narrow, list-buildable buyers (public directories, association member lists, tender award notices, Capterra review authors) + personalized cold email/LinkedIn + trade newsletters/communities. Avoid ideas that depend on SEO or content marketing to get first customers.
- AI should be the engine inside a system-of-record (deadlines, documents, history, reminders, approvals), so the customer's data accumulates in the product and the AI output is not the whole value.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025–2026 dataset on time to first $1k MRR; no Reddit first-hand retrospective with timing surfaced in searches.
- No verified churn benchmarks for sub-$10k MRR B2B products.

## 4. Concrete, buildable B2B SaaS ideas (8–10) with evidence, competitors, MVP, validation and scores

### Takeaway
Nine ideas outside the other researchers' main areas, ranked by fit. The strongest are (1) tender monitoring + AI bid drafting for small EU/UK suppliers, (2) grant pipeline + AI drafting for small nonprofits and freelance grant writers, and (3) association dues/renewal automation — each has a findable buyer list, an existing paid category proving willingness to pay, and an AI component wrapped in workflow. Scores (0–10) are the researcher's judgment based on the evidence above.

### Cited Findings
(Evidence per idea; everything not cited here is an inference listed below.)
- Tender/RFP: AI RFP tools priced ~$500–$900/month and aimed at proposal teams; DeepRFP explicitly targets small businesses and consultants — [SiftHub](https://www.sifthub.io/blog/autorfp-pricing-reviews-features-breakdown), [Inventive](https://www.inventive.ai/blog-posts/autorfp-ai-pricing-features-reviews), [Capterra DeepRFP](https://www.capterra.com/p/10028620/DeepRFP/).

- Credential/expiry tracking: cited as an unsexy niche with estimated (not reported) $7k–$18k MRR potential — [Redwerk](https://redwerk.com/blog/micro-saas-ideas-that-print-money/), [MicroGaps](https://www.microgaps.com/blog/boring-micro-saas-ideas-that-make-money).

### Inferences
Common assumptions for all ideas: running cost ~€20–€80/month at MVP stage (cheap VPS or Vercel/Fly free tier €0–20, Supabase free–€25, transactional email €0–15, LLM API €10–40 at low volume, domain €1/month). Stack: Next.js/Supabase or Django + Postgres, Stripe Billing, Resend/Postmark, an LLM API. All prices below are suggestions to test, not evidence.

#### Idea 1 — TenderPilot: public-tender monitoring + AI bid-drafting for small B2B suppliers (EU/UK/Nordics first)
- Target customer & buyer: 2–50-person companies that sell to the public sector (IT services, translation agencies, cleaning, training providers, consultancies, design/marketing agencies). Buyer: founder/MD or bid manager.
- Evidence: Existing AI RFP tools charge ~$500–$900/month and target proposal teams, leaving small bidders under-served; DeepRFP is moving into the small-business slot (see cited findings). EU TED, UK Find a Tender and Swedish/Nordic portals publish tenders openly (public-data advantage; verify API terms).
- Competitors & gap: AutoRFP.ai (~$500–$900/mo), Inventive, DeepRFP, Loopio (enterprise; price not retrieved), national tender-alert services (e.g., Tendium in Sweden — pricing not retrieved). Gap: cheap "find relevant tenders + extract requirements checklist + first-draft answers from your past bids" for companies bidding a few times a month.
- Suggested pricing: €79/month (alerts + requirement extraction, 5 tenders/month), €199/month (unlimited + AI drafting + answer library), annual discount.
- MVP (8 weeks, one dev): CPV-code/keyword tender alerts from TED + one national feed; PDF tender-document ingestion → requirement/compliance matrix and deadline list; "answer library" from uploaded past bids; AI first-draft per question with citations to library; DOCX export; Stripe billing.
- Distribution: co-founder cold emails companies that appear as winners/bidders in award notices (public lists by CPV code); LinkedIn outreach to bid managers; partnerships with bid-writing consultants.
- Risks: hallucinated answers in bids (mitigate with citations/human review); national portals vary; long tail of languages (start English-language UK/IE/Nordic tenders + EU TED English notices); DeepRFP-style competitors.
- 2-week validation: pull 200 recent award notices in 3 CPV codes, email 100 winners/runners-up with a free "tender digest + requirement checklist" for one live tender; target 10 calls and 3 pre-orders (€79 first month or €500 annual).
- Interview questions: (1) How many tenders did you bid on last year, and how many did you skip for lack of time? (2) Walk me through the last bid: hours spent and on what? (3) How do you find tenders today, and what do you pay for that? (4) Where do you store past answers, CVs and case studies? (5) What would a tool need to do for you to pay €100+/month?
- Scores: pain 8, WTP 8, low competition 6, ease of build 6, ease of selling (non-technical) 7, AI-dev fit 9, global potential 7.

#### Idea 2 — GrantDesk: grant pipeline, deadlines and AI drafting for small nonprofits and freelance grant writers
- Target & buyer: freelance grant writers/consultants managing 5–20 clients (B2B buyer who feels pain directly), and small nonprofits (<$2M budget). Buyer: consultant or executive director/development officer.
- Evidence: paid category proven (Instrumentl $179/mo+, Grantable $50/mo, 30k+ users claimed; multiple new Capterra entrants).
- Competitors & gap: Instrumentl (prospecting-heavy, $179+), Grantable ($50–$150), Granted AI, Imagagrant, ScriptaGrant. Gap: a multi-client workspace for freelance grant consultants (client folders, boilerplate library per client, funder deadlines calendar, report-due reminders, client-facing status page) — the consultant niche is less served than the nonprofit niche (verify).
- Suggested pricing: $39/month solo nonprofit; $99/month consultant (up to 10 clients); $199 agency.

**Cross-cutting lessons for this founder team (inferences drawn from Sections 1–3):**
- Pick a buyer you can list (public registers, award notices, association directories, marketplace reviewers) so the Swedish co-founder can run cold outreach from week 1; validation should start before code.
- Price per organization at $49–$199/month with a usage lever; low-ARPU ($15–$29) verticals need too many customers for a 15 h/week team.
- Wrap AI inside a system of record (deadlines, libraries, reminders, audit trails) to avoid the wrapper churn trap; the product's stored data (past bids, boilerplate, member lists, credentials) is the moat.
- Budget fits: €800/3 months covers infra (~€20–€80/month), domain, an email-sending tool and LLM credits, leaving ~€400–€550 for a lead list or data tool; no ads.
- Expect months, not weeks, to $1k MRR; aim for 3–5 paid design partners (annual pre-pay) by MVP week 8.
