# B2B SaaS Opportunities in "Unsexy" Operational Industries (Logistics, Construction, Trades, Facility Services, Small Manufacturing, Wholesale, Agri) — as of November 2026

Scope note: Research done 13 Nov 2026 with web search (about 22 queries). Most pricing and pain numbers come from vendor pages, vendor-sponsored surveys or affiliate comparison blogs. They are labelled as such. Treat them as directional, not audited. Anything dated before 2025 is marked [older].

Founder fit used for judging: one developer aged 18–19 in Moldova/Romania (AI/LLM, cybersecurity), a sales co-founder in Sweden who knows facility and construction services and sells in English, €800 budget for 3 months, about 15 h/week, MVP in about 8 weeks, recurring revenue, sold globally in English.

## 1. What are the costliest manual workflows, and can they be quantified?

### Takeaway
The best-quantified, highest-cost manual workflows are: (a) unbilled or unpaid change orders and T&M (time-and-materials) work in subcontracting, put at around 30% of project revenue in vendor surveys; (b) admin for quoting and invoicing in trades, at 5–8+ hours a week per owner; (c) missed or after-hours calls in home services, with 14–27% of calls unanswered; (d) manual entry of email/PDF orders at distributors, at about 6 minutes per order; and (e) subcontractor compliance paperwork, where small subs pay $450–900 a year per client platform. Quote-request handling in freight forwarding is clearly painful, because VCs are funding it heavily, but I found no independent number on time per quote.

### Cited Findings
**Construction: change orders and T&M**
- Procore survey (2023) [older; vendor]: respondents estimated that on average 32% of project revenue is lost to unbilled or unpaid change orders. Steel contractors lose 42%, concrete contractors 19%. — [Procore Jobsite](https://www.procore.com/jobsite/unbilled-and-unpaid-change-orders-are-hurting-your-revenue); [Procore blog](https://www.procore.com/blog/exploring-the-top-three-issues-for-specialty-contractors-protecting-profits-managing-labor-and-technology-usage)
- Clearstory x Dodge Construction Network 2026 subcontractor report [vendor]: 92% of subcontractors say their change orders are not always paid in full, only 6% say all are paid in full, and 66% wrote off change-order revenue in the last 12 months. Change orders are discounted by an average of 22% in negotiation. — [Clearstory SC industry report](https://www.clearstory.build/sc-industry-report)
- Clearstory: change orders and T&M make up 10–30% of a specialty contractor's annual revenue, and "up to 36% of T&M tags never make it from the field to the office." — [Clearstory blog](https://www.clearstory.build/construction-blog/how-change-orders-affect-subcontractor-cash-flow); [Clearstory webinar](https://www.clearstory.build/webinar-the-2026-state-of-change-orders-subcontactors)
- eSUB: subs often fund extra work up front and bill later, and GCs reclassify undocumented work as "original scope" at closeout. — [eSUB](https://esub.com/blog/change-order-management-for-trade-contractors-what-to-track-and-why-it-matters)

**Trades: admin, quoting and invoicing**
- Powered Now survey (June 2026, UK) [vendor]: an average of 5 h 20 min a week on quoting, invoicing and chasing payments, much of it in evenings and weekends. Another Powered Now figure is 7 h/week, or up to 48 days a year. — [Electrical Contracting News](https://electricalcontractingnews.com/news/industry-news/powered-now-tradespeople-report-growing-business-pressures/)
- HeyBRB survey (2026, n=167 UK small business owners incl. electricians, plumbers, builders): about 8 h/week lost to repetitive admin, or "ten working weeks a year." — [Electrical Times](https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/uk-tradespeople-losing-ten-working-weeks-a-year-to-admin-they-could-automate-survey-finds/)
- Admin overload is estimated to cost HVAC professionals over £17,000 a year (May 2026 survey). — [Installer Show](https://www.installershow.com/news/admin-overload-cost-over-17000-year-hvac-professionals-survey-finds)
- Tradify Pulse 2021 [older; vendor]: 39% of tradespeople spend more than 12 h/week on paperwork. — [Xero blog / Tradify](https://blog.xero.com/2021/02/right-tech-for-tradies-2021/)
- More than a third of respondents spend over 20 minutes writing one quote from scratch. — [Electrical Times](https://www.electricaltimes.co.uk/?p=34786)

**Trades: missed calls**
- Invoca [vendor]: 27% of calls to home-services businesses go unanswered. — [Invoca](https://invoca.com/blog/how-much-missed-sales-calls-cost-home-services-businesses)
- CallRail benchmark (Jan 2025): a 14% missed-call rate in home services (as reported by StealthAgents). — [StealthAgents](https://stealthagents.com/research/home-services-missed-call-revenue-statistics-2026)
- Value of a missed call: $200–500 in lost job revenue (Signpost), and up to $1,200 per missed call (Invoca, cited by Housecall Pro). — [Signpost](https://www.signpost.com/blog/how-much-business-do-contractors-lose-from-missed-calls/); [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/missed-calls/)

**Wholesale and distribution: order entry**
- Vendor claim: more than 30% of wholesale-distribution orders arrive through unstructured channels (email, text, chat, PDF) and need manual entry. Manual entry takes about 6 minutes per order. In Workd's worked example, a distributor handling 200 orders a day spends about $130k a year on it. — [Workd](https://www.workd.com/insights/articles/automating-order-entry/); [WizCommerce](https://wizcommerce.com/blog/ai-order-entry/)

- Distribution Strategy (Sept 2026): "AI moves into the distributor order desk as new tools target manual work." — [Distribution Strategy](https://distributionstrategy.com/2026/09/ai-moves-into-the-distributor-order-desk-as-new-tools-target-manual-work/)

**Subcontractor compliance paperwork**
- ISNetworld: about $875/year for a contractor or supplier, including a $125 setup fee (Capterra). Avetta: $799+/year plus a $249 setup fee (figure from Billy, a competitor). ERPResearch gives $450–900 per supplier per year. — [Capterra ISNetworld](https://www.capterra.com/compare/145737-173498/ISNetworld-vs-Blue-review); [Billy](https://billyforinsurance.com/resources/billy-vs-isnetworld-vs-avetta-subcontractor-prequalification/); [ERPResearch](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/supplier-management/avetta/pricing)

An older 2015 source gives SEK 10,000 + 2,000. — [Skatteverket FAQ](https://www.skatteverket.se/foretag/arbetsgivare/personalliggare/personalliggarebyggbranschen/fragorochsvarompersonalliggareibyggbranschen.4.361dc8c15312eff6fd6ba7.html); [FAR Online](https://www.faronline.se/dokument/rs_personalliggare/); [Företagarna 2015](https://www.foretagarna.se/nyheter/riks/2015/oktober/krav-pa-personalliggare-ocksa-hos-byggforetag)

**Freight forwarding: quote requests by email**
- Venture rounds in AI quote and email automation for forwarders and brokers: Nexcade (London) $2.5M pre-seed in Oct 2025, then a reported $6M seed in 2026 (one report says £4.5m, so the figure conflicts); 5U AI (Munich) $3.2M pre-seed; Vooma $16.6M (brokers); Aircon AI a reported $8M (date not confirmed). — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/10/07/nexcade-exits-stealth-with-25m-to-automate-global-freight-forwarding/); [The Loadstar](https://theloadstar.com/ls_press_release/nexcade-raises-6m-to-build-ai-agents-for-freight-forwarders/); [Air Cargo Week](https://aircargoweek.com/tech-start-up-5u-ai-raises-us3-2-million/); [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/vooma-grabs-16-6m-in-funding-as-brokers-prepare-for-market-swing); [AIM Media House](https://aimmediahouse.com/ai-startups/aircon-ai-wants-to-modernize-the-air-cargo-market-with-8m)

**Small manufacturing: RFQ quoting**
- Paperless Parts uses quote-based pricing aimed at "growing or established job shops," and cost is listed as a drawback for low-volume shops. Its "Wingman" AI extracts materials, tolerances and certifications from RFQ documents. — [SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/roundups/best-manufacturing-estimating-quoting-software/); [Paperless Parts](https://paperlessparts.com/feature-overview)
- QuotedPart is a smaller tool for CNC and sheet-metal shops. It takes RFQs from forwarded email and charges no per-user fees (pricing checked Oct 2026). — [ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/quotedpart)

### Inferences
- **Change-order leakage is the biggest, most defensible pain in dollar terms.** If a sub with €2M revenue writes off even 2–5% of revenue in undocumented extras, that is €40–100k a year, so a €50–100/month tool pays for itself many times over. The 30%+ "lost" numbers come from vendor surveys and are probably inflated, which is why I use 2–5% here.
- **Trades admin is real but heavily served.** The pain is clear (5–8 h/week), but Jobber, Housecall Pro, Tradify, Fergus and Powered Now all compete for it.
- **Missed calls make an easy ROI story.** A single recovered job ($200–500) pays for a month of service, so it is easy to sell, but the English-speaking market for it is crowded.
- **The heavy funding in freight-forwarding quote automation confirms the pain is real**, but it also means a solo founder faces well-funded competitors unless he targets a micro niche, such as small road forwarders in CEE/Nordics or a single lane type.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) time-and-motion data on minutes per freight quote at small forwarders.
- No reliable data on time spent on proof of delivery (POD) or CMR paperwork per shipment for small carriers.
- No reliable survey on how much time small EU contractors spend on subcontractor document checks. US COI data is mostly from vendors.
- The AGC 2024 statistic that 38% of subs see platform fees as a barrier is cited by a vendor and could not be verified against AGC itself.

## 2. Which tools exist, what do they cost, what do users complain about, and where are small companies underserved?

### Takeaway
The large horizontal and enterprise tools (ServiceTitan, Procore, Avetta/ISN, Aspire, CargoWise, Paperless Parts) price small operators out through quote-only pricing, implementation fees or per-supplier fees. Jobber and Housecall Pro serve small US/UK trades well at $29–399/month, so general field-service management is not a gap. The gaps are narrow workflow tools that are EU/Nordic-localised, flat-priced and cheap: subcontractor document compliance outside the US COI model, change-order capture for small subs, order entry for small distributors on SMB ERPs, and cleaning QA/client reporting.

### Inferences
- **Underserved segment 1:** small GCs and facility-services firms in the EU/UK/Nordics that need to collect and track many subcontractor documents with expiry dates (insurance, tax status such as Swedish F-skatt, safety certificates, A1 posted-worker forms, ID cards). Today they use Excel and email reminders. The US COI tools are built around ACORD 25 certificates and US brokers, and Avetta/ISN are the wrong tools for a 20-person contractor.
- **Underserved segment 2:** small subs (5–50 people) that cannot justify Procore or Clearstory but lose money on undocumented extras. A mobile capture-and-sign tool for under €100/mo is missing or thinly served.
- **Underserved segment 3:** distributors with 3–50 staff on Fortnox, Visma, Odoo, SmartBill or Excel. The AI order-entry vendors integrate with mid-market ERPs (Epicor, P21, NetSuite, SAP B1) and sell with enterprise sales cycles.
- **Underserved segment 4:** commercial cleaning firms with 5–40 sites, where Aspire is overkill and generic SafetyCulture lacks client-facing reporting.
- **Not underserved:** general field-service management for US/UK trades (Jobber/HCP), US English AI receptionists, and freight-forwarder AI quoting (VC-funded).

### Gaps
- No verified primary price pages for Swept, myCOI, TimoCom, Clearstory or Avoca.
- Market share of Excel/WhatsApp-only operators by country was not found. Assume it is high for firms under 20 staff, but I have no number.

## 3. Which regulatory deadlines in 2025–2027 force small operators to adopt software?

### Takeaway
The deadlines that truly force small operators to act in 2026–2027 are e-invoicing (Belgium in force since Jan 2026; France receiving from Sept 2026; Poland KSeF for the smallest taxpayers from Jan 2027; Germany issuing from Jan 2027 for turnover above €800k and for all businesses from Jan 2028) and EUDR (large and medium operators from 30 Dec 2026; micro and small from 30 Jun 2027, with simplified rules). eFTI, Digital Product Passports (DPP), CSRD and the Pay Transparency Directive are weak forcing functions for 3–100-person firms in this window.

### Cited Findings
**E-invoicing**
- Belgium: structured B2B e-invoicing for domestic transactions has been mandatory since 1 Jan 2026 for all VAT-registered businesses, built around Peppol. — [Sovos](https://sovos.com/taxapedia/?p=29); [Grant Thornton NL](https://www.grantthornton.nl/en/insights-en/tax/btw/recent-e-invoicing-developments-in-europe/)
- Poland KSeF: large taxpayers (above PLN 200M) from 1 Feb 2026, most VAT businesses from 1 Apr 2026, the smallest taxpayers from 1 Jan 2027. Penalties are reportedly suspended or deferred to 2027. — [OriginStamp](https://originstamp.com/en/blog/reader/eu-e-invoicing-mandates-timeline-2026-2030); [Taxually](https://www.taxually.com/blog/e-invoicing-deadlines-2027)
- France: all businesses must receive e-invoices from 1 Sep 2026. Large and mid-size firms issue from 1 Sep 2026, and SMEs/micro firms from 1 Sep 2027. — [Fintua](https://fintua.com/blog/einvoicing-mandate-watch-list-2026/); [Sovos](https://sovos.com/taxapedia/?p=29)
- Germany: all businesses must be able to receive e-invoices since 1 Jan 2025. Businesses with prior-year turnover above €800k must issue them from 1 Jan 2027, and all domestic B2B businesses from 1 Jan 2028. Micro businesses under €22k and invoices under €250 are exempt. EDI is allowed until the end of 2027. — [KPMG DE](https://kpmg.com/de/en/home/insights/2025/05/e-invoice-in-germany.html); [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.de/en/post/e-invoicing-in-germany-countdown-to-january-2027); [ClearTax](https://www.cleartax.com/de/en/e-invoicing-faqs-germany)
- German readiness: one in three companies has never sent an e-invoice, and only 42% send them regularly. SMEs are struggling the most. — [heise](https://heise.de/-11315725). Ebner Stolz: about 10% of companies are ready (headline). — [Ebner Stolz](https://www.ebnerstolz.de/en/what-we-offer/services/tax-advice/value-added-tax/action-required-e-invoicing-for-majority-of-businesses-109086.html)

**EUDR (deforestation)**
- After the Dec 2025 amendment, large and medium operators must comply from 30 Dec 2026, and micro and small operators from 30 Jun 2027. Only the operator first placing a product on the market submits a DDS; downstream operators and traders no longer do. Micro and small primary operators submit a one-off simplified declaration and may give a postal address instead of geolocation. A Commission simplification review was due by 30 Apr 2026. — [EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0237-eu-deforestation-regulation-application-postponed-to-30-december-2026); [Linklaters](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102ly55/eu-changes-to-deforestation-regulation-reach-the-finish-line); [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/alerts/2025/12/european-union-provisional-agreement-on-eudr-one-year-postponement); [Rödl](https://www.roedl.com/en/insights/eudr-update-simplified-due-diligence-postponed-effective-date/)
- The small-company extension covers only products not already covered by the EU Timber Regulation (EUTR). — [EY](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0237)
- Unverified claim (secondary source): the Commission says EUDR will not be reopened again. — [QIMA](https://blog.qima.com/sustainability/eudr-delay-updates-2026)

**eFTI (electronic freight transport information)**
- Regulation (EU) 2020/1056 applies in full from 9 Jul 2027. From then, all EU authorities must accept freight information submitted through certified eFTI platforms. Using it remains voluntary for businesses, and paper is still allowed. — [Traficom](https://traficom.fi/en/transport-system/connected-and-automated-transport/electronic-freight-transport-information); [Editel](https://www.editelgroup.com/knowledge-hub/publications/efti-in-the-final-stretch/); [ERTICO](https://ertico.com/efti-regulation-digital-transformation-of-eu-freight-transport)

**Digital Product Passport (ESPR)**
- Battery passport from 18 Feb 2027 for LMT (light means of transport) batteries, industrial batteries above 2 kWh and EV batteries. The textiles, tyres and aluminium delegated acts are expected around 2027, with realistic textile compliance from 2028. Furniture follows in 2028, mattresses and ICT in 2029. — [EU Circular Economy Platform](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/node/10371); [Renoon](https://www.renoon.com/blog/the-timeline-of-digital-product-passport-regulation-in-eu-espr-from-product-groups-to-rollout-schedule); [EC DPP](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport_en)

**CSRD value-chain requests (Omnibus I, agreed Dec 2025)**
- Suppliers with fewer than 1,000 employees ("protected undertakings") can refuse sustainability-data requests that go beyond the VSME (Voluntary SME) standard, and a self-declaration of size is enough. The cap does not affect requests made under other laws (CS3D, Forced Labour Regulation). Sources disagree on the CSRD scope threshold (1,000 employees + €450M turnover versus higher). — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/agreement-on-the-csrd-cs3d-omnibus-package-key-changes-and-implications); [De Brauw](https://www.debrauw.com/articles/omnibus-now-final-agreement-reached-on-directive-amending-csrd-and-csddd); [PwC LU](https://www.pwc.lu/en/sustainable-finance/eu-finalises-agreement-on-omnibus.html)

**EU Pay Transparency Directive**
- The transposition deadline of 7 Jun 2026 was missed by most member states. Only about 4 (Italy, Lithuania, Malta, Slovakia) had fully transposed it. Denmark proposes 1 Jan 2027, Sweden and Estonia sought postponement, and the Commission rejected extensions. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/07/01/eu-pay-transparency-directive-2026-employer-compliance); [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/did-member-states-meet-deadline-status-implementation-eu-pay-transparency); [Remote Work Europe](https://remoteworkeurope.eu/news/2026/eu-pay-transparency-directive-deadline-missed-23-of-27-states)

**Sweden: construction personalliggare** (ongoing, not new): see the fines in Q1. — [Skatteverket](https://www.skatteverket.se/foretag/arbetsgivare/personalliggare/personalliggarebyggbranschen.4.361dc8c15312eff6fd6ba7.html)

### Inferences
- **E-invoicing is the strongest forcing function**, but accounting suites (sevDesk, lexoffice, DATEV, Billit, Fortnox, SmartBill) will absorb most of the demand. A standalone e-invoice converter is a commodity. It works better as a feature inside a trades invoicing or order tool, for example "voice job notes → Peppol/XRechnung/KSeF invoice."
- **EUDR has a real but narrowing window.** Downstream traders no longer file DDSs and small primary operators get one-off declarations, so the remaining market is small EU importers that first place coffee, cocoa, timber, rubber, soy, palm or cattle products on the market, before 30 Jun 2027. That is a one-time compliance rush with regulatory-change risk.
- **eFTI is not worth pursuing for this team:** it is voluntary for businesses and platforms need certification, which is too heavy for one developer.
- **DPP is too early and too enterprise-focused** in 2026–27. Batteries mean large OEMs.
- **CSRD pressure on small suppliers is now capped at VSME.** That makes a tiny "VSME answer pack" product possible, but buyers are less likely to push small suppliers hard.
- **Pay transparency affects HR/payroll vendors** and has mostly missed its deadline. It is not a fit.

### Gaps
- Romania e-Factura (B2B mandatory since 2024, B2C since 2025) and its Moldovan equivalent were not re-verified in this session.
- I could not verify a primary Commission source confirming that EUDR will not be reopened again.
- Pay Transparency reporting thresholds (150+ employees from 2027; 100–149 from 2031) come from the directive text as I remember it and were not re-verified here.

#### Idea 1: SubDocs — AI tracking of subcontractor and supplier documents and expiries (EU/UK/Nordic first)
- **Customer and buyer:** GCs, facility-management and facility-services firms (cleaning, security, property maintenance) with 10–100 staff that use 10–200 subcontractors. The buyer is the owner, operations manager or HSE manager. The co-founder knows this world (Swedish construction and facility services).
- **Pain:** Insurance certificates, tax registration (F-skatt / VAT), safety training certificates, ID06, A1 posted-worker forms and work permits all expire. If they lapse, the firm faces fines (Swedish personalliggare SEK 12,500 + 2,500/person), uninsured losses or failed client audits. Today this is tracked in Excel plus email chasing. Small subs hate paying $450–900/yr to Avetta/ISN.
- **Competitors and gap:** US-focused COI tools (Billy, myCOI, CertCove $99/mo, SubDoc $14/mo, SmartCompliance $1k+/yr). Enterprise prequalification (Avetta, ISN) charges the suppliers. The gap is a free-for-subs, flat-priced, multi-document, EU-localised tool where AI reads any PDF or photo and pulls out type, holder, expiry and coverage.
- **Suggested pricing:** €49/mo (up to 25 subs), €99/mo (100), €199/mo (unlimited plus client audit portal). Free for subcontractors.
- **Running cost:** about €20–60/mo at under 50 customers (LLM extraction is a few cents per document).
- **MVP (8 weeks):** (1) a magic-link upload portal for subs, plus email-forward intake; (2) LLM + OCR extraction of document type, company, policy number, expiry and amounts, with human confirmation; (3) a dashboard per sub (green/amber/red); (4) automatic reminder emails to subs at 30/14/0 days, in their language; (5) CSV import of the subcontractor list; (6) one-click PDF audit pack per project or client; (7) audit log. Security (encryption, GDPR, EU hosting) becomes a selling point that matches the developer's cybersecurity skills.
- **Risks:** document types differ by country, so start with Sweden and the UK. Some buyers want a checklist in Excel. Larger firms may require integrations with construction ERPs. GDPR applies to ID documents.
- **2-week no-code validation:** Days 1–3: co-founder calls 20 Swedish/UK facility and construction firms from his network; a landing page (Carrd) shows a "send us your sub list, we'll return a compliance status report free." Days 4–10: do 5 "concierge" audits by hand (or with ChatGPT plus a spreadsheet), where firms forward their documents and get back an expiry report and reminder drafts. Days 11–14: ask for a €49/mo pre-order or a letter of intent. Success means 3 or more paying pre-orders.
- **Interview questions:** (1) How many subcontractors or suppliers did you use last year, and how do you track their insurance and certificates today? (2) When did an expired document last cause a problem (fine, client audit, claim)? What did it cost? (3) Who spends time chasing documents, and how many hours a month? (4) What do your clients or main contractors ask you to prove, and how often? (5) What have you tried (Excel, Avetta, ID06 tools), and why did you stop or keep it?

#### Idea 2: ExtraWork — change-order and T&M capture with client e-sign for small subcontractors
- **Customer and buyer:** specialty subs with 5–50 staff (electrical, HVAC, drywall, painting, steel, scaffolding) in the UK, Nordics, US and Australia. The buyer is the owner or project manager. Field users are the foremen.
- **Pain:** 92% of subs are not always paid in full for change orders, 66% wrote off revenue in the last 12 months, and up to 36% of T&M tags never reach the office (Clearstory/Dodge 2026, vendor data). Extras are agreed verbally or on WhatsApp.
- **Competitors and gap:** Clearstory (enterprise), eSUB, Procore ($4.5k–30k/yr), Fieldwire ($39–89/user). The gap is a WhatsApp-simple mobile flow: the foreman speaks or types "extra: moved 6 sockets per site manager request, 2 men 4 hours + materials". AI structures it into a priced T&M tag with photos, sends a link to the GC's site manager for e-sign, and logs it for invoicing.
- **Suggested pricing:** €39/mo (3 field users), €99/mo (15 users), €199/mo (unlimited plus rates library).
- **Running cost:** about €20–50/mo (speech-to-text plus LLM per tag is cheap; SMS/email notifications).
- **MVP:** PWA (progressive web app) with voice-to-text and photo capture; AI pulls out labour hours, materials and reason; a rate card per company; a signable link for the GC with timestamp, geotag and PDF; status board (draft, sent, signed, invoiced, disputed); monthly export to CSV/accounting; email reminders for unsigned tags.
- **Risks:** GC site managers may refuse to sign. Adoption depends on foremen actually using it. The pain is lower in markets with strong contracts (Nordic AB04/ABT06) and higher in UK and US.
- **2-week validation:** Post in r/Construction and r/electricians plus LinkedIn; co-founder calls 15 Swedish and UK subs. Offer a Typeform plus PDF template "digital T&M tag" for free, and see whether foremen use it on real jobs within a week. Ask 5 firms for a €39/mo pre-order.
- **Interview questions:** (1) How much extra work did you do last year that you never got paid for, roughly? (2) How does a foreman record extra work today, and how does it reach the office? (3) How often does the GC dispute a variation, and on what grounds? (4) How long from doing the extra work to invoicing it? (5) Would your site managers sign on a phone link? Why or why not?

#### Idea 3: CleanProof — QA inspections and automatic client reports for cleaning and facility services
- **Customer and buyer:** commercial cleaning and facility-services companies with 10–100 staff and 5–60 sites. The buyer is the owner or area manager. This matches the co-founder's domain.
- **Pain:** contracts are lost because clients see no proof of quality. Inspections happen on paper, and supervisors write monthly reports by hand.
- **Competitors and gap:** Swept (about $77+/mo, location tiers), CleanTelligent, Janitorial Manager, Aspire (enterprise, 6–12 month setup), SafetyCulture ($24/user/mo, generic). The gap is AI-generated client-facing monthly quality reports (photos, scores, issue resolution) plus QR "rate this restroom" feedback, at a flat price.
- **Suggested pricing:** €59/mo (10 sites), €149/mo (40 sites).
- **Running cost:** about €15–40/mo (photo storage is the main cost).
- **MVP:** checklist builder from templates; mobile inspections with photos and scores; QR feedback per site; issue tickets assigned to cleaners; AI monthly PDF/email report per client; client read-only portal.
- **Risks:** crowded mid-tier (Swept, CleanTelligent, SweepOps-type indie tools at $20–99). Weaker AI moat. Cleaning firms have low margins.
- **2-week validation:** The co-founder offers 5 Nordic cleaning firms a free "monthly quality report" built by hand from their photos (Google Forms + ChatGPT). Measure whether their clients react, and whether the firms will pay €59/mo to keep it.
- **Interview questions:** (1) How do you prove cleaning quality to clients today? (2) When did you last lose or nearly lose a contract over quality complaints? (3) Who does inspections, how often, and on what? (4) How long does monthly client reporting take? (5) What software have you tried, and why did it stick or not?

#### Idea 4: OrderDesk Lite — email/PDF/WhatsApp order entry for small wholesalers (Fortnox, Visma, Odoo, SmartBill, Excel)
- **Customer and buyer:** distributors and wholesalers with 3–50 staff (food, building materials, industrial supplies, agri inputs) receiving 20–200 orders a day by email, PDF, fax-to-email or WhatsApp. The buyer is the owner or sales-ops lead.
- **Pain:** about 6 minutes of manual entry per order, more than 30% of orders unstructured, plus wrong SKUs and pricing errors (vendor figures).
- **Competitors and gap:** Proton.ai, Y Meadows, WizCommerce, Moblico, Advantive Lucy, Tungsten, all aimed at mid-market or enterprise ERPs. The gap is SMB ERPs and accounting tools in the Nordics, CEE and UK, with self-serve onboarding.
- **Suggested pricing:** €99/mo (500 orders), €249/mo (2,000 orders).
- **Running cost:** about €30–80/mo (LLM per order; WhatsApp API fees).
- **MVP:** a dedicated inbound email address; LLM extraction of customer, lines, quantities and dates; fuzzy SKU matching against an uploaded product CSV; review screen; export to CSV or one ERP API (Fortnox first for Sweden); confirmation email back to the customer.
- **Risks:** ERP integrations eat development time, and accuracy expectations are high. The mid-market players may move down-market.
- **2-week validation:** Find 10 Swedish or Romanian wholesalers. Ask for 50 real historical order emails, run them through GPT by hand, and show accuracy plus time saved. Get 2 letters of intent.
- **Interview questions:** (1) How many orders a day arrive, and through which channels? (2) Who types them in, and how long per order? (3) How often are there mistakes, and what does one cost? (4) What ERP or accounting system do you use? (5) Have you tried customer portals or EDI, and why did customers not adopt them?

#### Idea 5: AfterHours — multilingual AI call answering and booking for EU/Nordic trades and cleaning firms
- **Customer and buyer:** plumbers, electricians, HVAC firms, locksmiths and cleaning companies with 2–30 staff. Start with Swedish, Romanian and German speakers, not US English.
- **Pain:** 14–27% of calls missed, $200–500 per lost job (US data).
- **Competitors and gap:** heavily crowded in US English (Rosie $49, Sameday $449, Avoca, Goodcall, dozens more). The gap is local languages and integration with EU booking or field-service tools (Bygglet, Fortnox, Tradify).
- **Suggested pricing:** €79–199/mo.
- **Running cost:** variable. Voice AI plus telephony at an estimated €0.05–0.15/min (not verified), so margin depends on call volume.
- **MVP:** forwarding number, voice agent that takes the name, address, problem and urgency, SMS summary to the owner, calendar booking link, transcript dashboard.
- **Risks:** competition, telephony complexity in each country, voice quality in Swedish and Romanian, and per-minute costs.
- **2-week validation:** Offer 10 Swedish trades an "answering service trial" with call forwarding to a voice-agent platform (Vapi/Retell no-code). Measure calls captured and willingness to pay.
- **Interview questions:** (1) How many calls do you miss in a week, and when? (2) What happens to the missed callers? (3) What is a typical job worth? (4) Have you used an answering service, and what did it cost? (5) Would customers accept talking to an AI in your language?

#### Idea 6: QuoteInbox — email RFQ-to-quote copilot for small road and LTL forwarders and job shops (pick one)
- **Customer and buyer:** road freight forwarders and brokers with 3–30 staff in CEE and the Nordics, or small CNC/sheet-metal job shops.
- **Pain:** quote requests arrive as messy emails. Win rates depend on speed. Funding rounds (Nexcade, 5U AI, Vooma, Aircon AI) confirm the pain.
- **Competitors and gap:** funded AI forwarder agents, CargoAi, Paperless Parts (quote-based, costly for small shops), QuotedPart. The gap is micro operators who price from their own Excel rate sheet.
- **Pricing:** €99–299/mo. **Running cost:** about €30–60/mo.
- **MVP:** inbox connection; RFQ field extraction (origin, destination, weight, pallets, dates, ADR/dangerous goods); lookup against the customer's rate-table CSV; reply draft; win/loss tracking.
- **Risks:** heavy competition, rate logic differs by company, and the co-founder has no logistics network.
- **Validation:** 10 forwarders on Trans.eu/LinkedIn. Process 30 of their past RFQs by hand.
- **Interview questions:** How many quote requests a day? Average time per quote? Share answered within 1 hour? Where do rates live? What would faster quoting win you?

#### Idea 7: VoiceInvoice — job notes by voice or WhatsApp turned into compliant e-invoices (Peppol, XRechnung, KSeF) for micro trades
- **Customer and buyer:** 1–10-person trades in Belgium, Germany and Poland. Mandate dates: Belgium Jan 2026, Poland small taxpayers Jan 2027, Germany issuing for turnover above €800k Jan 2027 and for all businesses 2028.
- **Pain:** 5–8 h/week of admin, and PDF invoices become non-compliant (in Germany, from 2027 a PDF can invalidate the customer's VAT deduction).
- **Competitors and gap:** Jobber, HCP, Tradify and Powered Now (field-service management); sevDesk, lexoffice and Billit (accounting with e-invoicing). The gap is narrow: capturing invoices by voice or WhatsApp.
- **Pricing:** €19–39/mo. **Running cost:** about €20/mo plus Peppol access-point fees (unverified).
- **Risks:** crowded and low-priced, local language and tax rules in each country, and existing tools add AI quickly.
- **Interview questions:** How do you invoice today? How long per invoice? Do you know your e-invoice deadline? What do you use for accounting? Would you send a voice note to create an invoice?

#### Idea 8: EUDR Lite — due diligence statement (DDS) workflow for micro and small EU importers (window up to 30 Jun 2027)
- **Customer and buyer:** small importers of specialty coffee, cocoa or chocolate, tropical timber and furniture, and rubber goods that first place products on the EU market.
- **Pain:** they must collect supplier geolocation and risk evidence and file DDSs in TRACES.
- **Competitors and gap:** TraceX €40–50 per supplier per month + €5k setup, LiveEO €15 per extra supplier per month, FlyPix €25–62.50/mo, osapiens SME starter, IntegrityNext, Coolset. The gap is a flat €29–79/mo self-serve tool for under 20 suppliers.
- **Running cost:** about €20/mo.
- **MVP:** supplier questionnaire link (plot GPS or postal address for small primary producers); document upload with AI extraction; risk checklist by country benchmark; DDS draft and TRACES submission guide; record-keeping.
- **Risks:** regulatory churn (it has been amended twice), the market shrank with the downstream exemption, it is a one-time rush rather than lasting recurring revenue, and specialist consultants compete.
- **Interview questions:** Which EUDR commodities do you import and from how many suppliers? Do you know whether you are the "operator"? What have you done so far? Who is helping you (consultant, software)? What budget have you set?
