# B2B SaaS Opportunities in Cybersecurity & Regulatory Compliance for SMBs (5–250 employees), late 2026

Direct page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked in this session for nearly every domain tried (underdefense.com, jonesday.com, vendr.com, digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu, hn.algolia.com), so every finding below comes from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. ## Q1. Which regulations create new mandatory work for SMBs or their suppliers in 2025–2027, what must they do, and by when?

### Takeaway
The hardest new legal deadline that is already live is the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) reporting duty. Since 11 Sept 2026, every manufacturer of a "product with digital elements" sold in the EU must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents within 24h/72h through ENISA's Single Reporting Platform. Full CRA obligations, including SBOMs, follow on 11 Dec 2027. NIS2 is now law in most member states and pushes security requirements down to SMB suppliers through contracts. Several other regimes are softer or delayed: the AI Act high-risk rules (moved to Dec 2027/Aug 2028), the HIPAA Security Rule overhaul (pushed to at least July 2027), and PCI DSS SAQ A script requirements (removed for SAQ A merchants). That shifts the best "forced-spend" opportunities toward CRA, NIS2 supply chain and cyber insurance.

### Cited Findings

**EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847)**
- Article 14 reporting duties started on 11 Sept 2026. ENISA's Single Reporting Platform (SRP) went live the same day, and reports go to the relevant national CSIRT and ENISA at once. — [Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/cra-single-reporting-platform-launched-reporting-requirements-apply-with-immediate-effect); [NCSC Ireland](https://ncsc.gov.ie/cra/); [Mondaq, "It's live"](https://www.mondaq.com/compliance/1843102/its-live-the-cyber-resilience-act-reporting-is-mandatory-as-of-today-11-september-2026)
- Manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents: a 24-hour early warning, then a 72-hour notification, then a final report whose timing depends on the category. — [usd.de](https://www.usd.de/en/cyber-resilience-act-reporting-obligations-sep-2026/); [Eversheds Sutherland](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/luxembourg/insights/cyber-resilience-act-september-2026)
- The Commission published CRA reporting guidance on 27 July 2026. — [Eversheds Sutherland](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/luxembourg/insights/cyber-resilience-act-september-2026); official page: [EC CRA reporting](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting)
- Fines reach up to €15M or 2.5% of global annual turnover. — [advisori.de](https://www.advisori.de/blog/cra-single-reporting-platform-srp-registration) (secondary source; check against the Regulation text)
- Full CRA obligations apply from 11 Dec 2027. That includes a machine-readable SBOM covering at least top-level dependencies, kept up to date and supplied to market-surveillance authorities on request. The SBOM does not have to be published. — [SafeDep](https://safedep.io/sbom-and-eu-cra-cyber-resilience-act); [advisori.de 12-month countdown](https://www.advisori.de/blog/cra-december-2027-full-compliance-countdown)
- ENISA SME CRA Survey (fieldwork Feb–Mar 2026, 194 organisations, 31 countries): 66% of respondents had heard of the CRA. Incident response and product lifecycle management was the weakest area, with an average maturity of 2.6/5. 73% asked for technical documentation templates and 71% for secure-development templates. 142 respondents said they need financial support. A voluntary survey probably over-represents engaged firms. — [ENISA news](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/where-do-smes-stand-in-preparing-for-the-cyber-resilience-act); [ENISA report PDF](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/SME%20CRA%20survey%20report.pdf); [Martel Innovate summary](https://martel-innovate.com/?p=363037); [cyberresilienceact.eu commentary](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/de/news/enisa-sme-cra-survey-high-awareness-low-readiness.html)
- ENISA says it is building practical guidance, tools and support for SMEs as part of the Commission's SME cybersecurity strategy. — [ENISA report PDF](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/SME%20CRA%20survey%20report.pdf)

**NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555)**
- Transposition: 20/27 member states by early Jan 2026 ([Wavestone](https://www.wavestone.com/en/insight/nis-2-european-countries-transposing-directive/)) and 22/27 with a law in force by 18 July 2026 (tracker cited in search). Luxembourg's law took effect 10 May 2026. On 8 July 2026 the Commission referred Ireland, Spain, France and the Netherlands to the CJEU for incomplete transposition. — [Cullen International](https://www.cullen-international.com/news/2026/05/How-are-EU-member-states-transposing-NIS2-.html); [Fieldfisher NIS2 across the EU, June 2026](https://res.cloudinary.com/fieldfisher/image/upload/v1783691713/PDFs/NIS2_across_the_EU_JUNE_2026_wokdct.pdf); [nisd2.eu tracker](https://nisd2.eu/en/wiki/timelines-and-status/nis2-eu-tracker). Counts differ between trackers.
- The Netherlands reportedly gave about 8,000 organisations five weeks to prepare. — [ManageEngine Insights](https://www.manageengine.com/insights/it-security/netherlands-gave-8000-organizations-five-weeks-prepare-nis2-shorter-window-compliance-constraint)
- Article 21(2)(d) requires in-scope entities to manage supply-chain security for their *direct* suppliers and service providers. Sources say this works through contract clauses (security requirements, audit rights, incident notification). In effect, "NIS2 regulates your clients, not you", but the contractual requirements are "just as binding". — [nisd2.eu supplier clauses](https://nisd2.eu/en/wiki/law-and-consequences/nis2-supplier-contract-clauses); [Computing.co.uk, "NIS2 turns supplier security into a contract negotiation"](https://www.computing.co.uk/opinion/2026/nis2-turns-supplier-security-into-a-contract-negotiation); [GlobalCert guide](https://www.glocertinternational.com/resources/guides/nis2-supply-chain-security-third-party-risk/)
- Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690 turns Article 21 into 150+ specific controls for certain digital service providers and managed service providers, including MSPs, cloud and hosting providers. — cited via search summary of [Bastion](https://bastion.tech/learn/nis2/supply-chain-security)
- Member states may go beyond the baseline (Art. 5), so obligations differ by country. — [GlobalCert country variance](https://www.glocertinternational.com/resources/articles/nis2-country-transposition-what-varies/)

**DORA (financial sector, applies since 17 Jan 2025)**
- Financial entities must keep a register of information covering *all* ICT third-party contractual arrangements, including subcontracting chains. National submissions were due around March 2026 (BaFin 30 Mar 2026; DNB March; CSSF portal opened 11 Feb 2026). — [AFM](https://www.afm.nl/en/sector/themas/belangrijke-europese-wet--en-regelgeving/dora/informatieregister); [CSSF](https://www.cssf.lu/en/2026/02/dora-submission-timeframe-for-register-of-information-edesk-portal-open-as-of-11-february-2026/); [DNB](https://www.dnb.nl/en/sector-news/supervision-2026/q1/dora-reporting-dora-registers-of-information-in-march-2026/); [FSMA limited update 2026](https://www.fsma.be/en/news/dora-register-information-third-party-ict-service-providers-limited-update-2026)
- In Nov 2025 the ESAs designated the first 19 critical ICT third-party providers. — search summary of [Sedric](https://www.sedric.ai/blog/dora-third-party-risk)
- Small ICT vendors to financial firms should expect contract demands: the right to object to subcontractors, advance notice of changes, audit rights. — [sota.io developer guide](https://sota.io/blog/eu-dora-ict-third-party-provider-register-contractual-requirements-saas-developer-guide-2026) (vendor blog)

**EU AI Act (as amended by the Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026)**
- High-risk Annex III obligations moved to 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I embedded systems to 2 Aug 2028. Prohibitions, GPAI and Article 50 transparency rules kept their dates. Article 50(2) watermarking/synthetic-content disclosure moved to 2 Dec 2026. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/27/the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force-today-102nedo)
- The Article 4 AI literacy duty (applies since 2 Feb 2025) was softened from "ensure a sufficient level" to "take measures to support AI literacy", which makes it an obligation of effort. — [euaiact.com](https://www.euaiact.com/digital-omnibus-ai); [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/)

**PCI DSS 4.0.1**
- From 31 Mar 2025, SAQ A merchants no longer have requirements 6.4.3 (payment-page script inventory/authorisation) and 11.6.1 (change/tamper detection). These were replaced by an eligibility criterion that the site is "not susceptible to attacks from scripts". Both requirements still apply to SAQ D merchants and onsite-assessed merchants. — [Adyen docs](https://docs.adyen.com/pt/development-resources/pci-dss-compliance-guide/saq-a-eligibility); [HUMAN Security](https://www.humansecurity.com/learn/blog/pci-dss-4-0-1-updates-to-browser-script-requirements/); [Report URI FAQ](https://blog.report-uri.com/pci-dss-4-0-1-saq-a-and-client-side-security-a-plain-english-faq/)

**HIPAA (US)**
- The Security Rule overhaul (NPRM Jan 2025) would make MFA and encryption required instead of addressable. HHS has pushed final action to at least July 2027 and moved it to "long-term actions". The current rule still applies, and pages claiming it is "finalized" are unreliable. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/feds-push-back-hipaa-security-rule-overhaul-july-2027); [Paubox](https://www.paubox.com/blog/hhs-pushes-final-hipaa-security-rule-update-to-2027)

**US state privacy laws**
- Indiana, Kentucky and Rhode Island took effect 1 Jan 2026. Oklahoma and Louisiana follow 1 Jan 2027, Alabama 1 May 2027 and Vermont 1 Jan 2028 (one tracker). About 19–20 states have comprehensive laws, depending on how they are counted. Thresholds are consumer-volume based (Indiana/Kentucky: 100k consumers, or 25k with ≥50% of revenue from data sales; Rhode Island reportedly as low as 35k), so many SMBs fall below them. — [Enzuzo tracker](https://www.enzuzo.com/blog/us-state-privacy-laws); [Baker Donelson](https://bakerdonelson.com/privacy-laws-ring-in-the-new-year-state-requirements-expand-across-the-us-in-2026); [Ketch](https://www.ketch.com/blog/posts/us-privacy-laws-2026)

**UK Cyber Essentials (relevant to English-speaking sales)**
- Version 3.3 ("Danzell" question set) took effect on 27 April 2026 and reportedly tightens MFA. Basic certification costs about £300–£650+VAT depending on organisation size. A "Cyber Essentials First Step Fund" offers eligible SMEs a 75% discount (as little as £80, capped at £125+VAT). — [Computing.co.uk](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/security/smes-offered-75-percent-discount-cyber-essentials); [Forensic Control](https://forensiccontrol.com/guides/how-much-does-cyber-essentials-cost/); [IASME](https://iasme.co.uk/cyber-essentials/?p=854)

**Cyber insurance (de facto mandatory controls)**
- Underwriters treat MFA (remote and email access, privileged accounts), EDR (they ask for tool names and deployment percentages) and tested backups as baseline controls. More and more, they want evidence such as console exports instead of yes/no answers. — [UnderDefense readiness checklist](https://underdefense.com/blog/cyber-insurance-readiness-checklist/); [JMCO](https://technology.jmco.com/cyber-insurance-and-your-it-environment-what-underwriters-now-require/); [Cyber Advisors 2026](https://blog.cyberadvisors.com/whats-new-in-cyber-insurance-2026) (vendor/MSP marketing)

### Inferences
- CRA is the strongest new "legally forced, deadline-driven" trigger in 2026–2027. It hits *small hardware and software makers* (IoT, embedded, desktop/mobile apps, SaaS components), not typical office SMBs. ENISA's own data shows low maturity in exactly the area that is now mandatory (incident response/lifecycle, 2.6/5), plus a stated demand for templates.
- NIS2 creates *indirect* demand. Millions of SMB suppliers get questionnaires and contract clauses from in-scope customers without being regulated themselves. That favours cheap, self-serve "answer my customer" tools over full GRC.
- Delays in the AI Act high-risk rules and HIPAA, and the SAQ A carve-out in PCI, mean product ideas built on those deadlines alone are weaker in 2026–2027 than marketing pages suggest.
- Cyber insurance works as a private regulator for US/UK SMBs. Its control demands (MFA coverage, EDR, backups) are concrete and checkable through Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace APIs.

### Gaps
- Exact CRA final-report timelines (14 days / 1 month) and SME/microenterprise relief could not be checked against the Regulation text because the fetch was blocked.
- No reliable count of SMEs indirectly affected by NIS2 supply-chain clauses was found.
- No primary text of the Cyber Essentials v3.3 changes was retrieved.
- DORA proportionality relief for small ICT subcontractors was not confirmed.

## Q2. What do SMBs currently use and pay, and what do they complain about?

### Takeaway
Compliance automation platforms (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe) typically cost $7.5k–$20k+/year before audit fees of $10k–$50k, and the most frequent complaint is price and scaling cost. Point tools exist cheaply in some categories: DMARC from $0–36/mo, phishing training at $1.6–3.75/user/mo but with 25-seat minimums. Others stay expensive: attack surface monitoring at $119–1,599/mo, trust centers at $5k–15k/yr. The gaps are a price band under $200/month and the 5–50 employee segment.

### Inferences
- There is a clear price gap between free DIY options and $5k+/yr platforms. A $29–199/mo self-serve product, sold by credit card without a sales call, is credible in questionnaires/trust pages, cyber-insurance readiness, NIS2 supplier evidence and CRA reporting.
- DMARC tooling is commoditised (free–$36/mo with many competitors), so a pure DMARC product is a poor bet. DMARC/SPF/DKIM checks work better as a feature inside a broader MSP or SMB "security posture" report.
- Phishing simulation is crowded, and Microsoft bundles it in E5. The only gaps are no-minimum pricing for under 25 seats and local-language content, both of which are being eroded.
- MSPs are a strong distribution channel: they already resell per-client tools, want compliance revenue, and complain about manual reporting.

### Gaps
- Vendor list prices for Thoropass, Scytale, Hyperproof, Huntress and 1Password were not retrieved in this session. Search budget went to higher-signal items.
- Reddit (r/msp, r/sysadmin, r/smallbusiness) and Hacker News threads could not be read directly. Practitioner sentiment here relies on G2 aggregates and vendor blogs.
- No pricing was found for the enterprise shadow-AI tools (e.g. Harmonic, Prompt Security, Nightfall) or SaaS-management/offboarding tools (Torii, Zluri, BetterCloud, CloudEagle).

## Q3. Where are the gaps (unmet, documented pain plus an affordable-tool gap)?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced gaps are: (1) CRA reporting and vulnerability-handling workflows for small product makers, live since Sept 2026 with documented low readiness; (2) cheap, AI-assisted answers to supplier/security questionnaires, now driven by NIS2/DORA flow-down as well as SOC 2 demands; (3) evidence-based cyber-insurance readiness, with MFA gaps behind most denials and carriers asking for proof; (4) MSP-friendly external posture reports; (5) shadow-AI governance for SMBs; (6) offboarding/access hygiene in small firms.

### Cited Findings
- 88% of SMB breaches in the 2025 Verizon DBIR involved ransomware (39% at large organisations). ### Inferences
- A "small product maker + CRA" gap exists: enterprise SBOM vendors (Black Duck, Revenera) and law firms serve large manufacturers, while ENISA data shows small makers want templates and cheap workflows.
- Because claims come mostly from BEC and FTF, and MFA gaps drive denials, a cheap tool that proves MFA coverage, checks mailbox rules and email authentication, and fills in the insurer questionnaire maps directly onto money SMBs lose.
- Narrow "monitoring"-style tools by solo founders reach low four figures to low five figures of MRR. That is a realistic ceiling for year one and helps set expectations.

### Gaps
- No SMB-only cut of the Coalition or DBIR data was retrieved.
- Volume data on how many security questionnaires an average SMB receives per year was not found from a credible survey.

## Q4. Concrete product ideas: customer, evidence, competitors, pricing, cost, MVP, risks, validation, interview questions

### Takeaway
Eight ideas are documented below, in rough order of attractiveness for this founder pair. Ideas 1 (CRA reporting kit), 4 (cyber-insurance readiness) and 2 (questionnaire/trust autopilot) score highest. Idea 1 has the best legal forcing function and technical fit. Idea 4 is the easiest to sell to non-technical buyers in the US/UK. Idea 2 has the broadest global market but the most competition.

### Cited Findings

#### Idea 1: "CRA Reporting Desk": vulnerability and incident reporting workflow plus SBOM for small connected-product and software makers
- **Customer / buyer:** EU and non-EU SMEs (5–250 staff) that sell products with digital elements in the EU: IoT/embedded device makers, smart-home and industrial gadget firms, desktop/mobile software vendors. The buyer is the CTO, head of engineering or a founder. A secondary channel is CRA consultants and notified-body-adjacent advisers, who could resell it.
- **Evidence of pain:** Reporting has been mandatory since 11 Sept 2026, with a 24h early warning and 72h notification through the ENISA SRP ([Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/cra-single-reporting-platform-launched-reporting-requirements-apply-with-immediate-effect); [usd.de](https://www.usd.de/en/cyber-resilience-act-reporting-obligations-sep-2026/)). Fines reach €15M or 2.5% of turnover ([advisori](https://www.advisori.de/blog/cra-single-reporting-platform-srp-registration)). SBOMs are mandatory from 11 Dec 2027 ([SafeDep](https://safedep.io/sbom-and-eu-cra-cyber-resilience-act)). In ENISA's SME survey, maturity in incident response/lifecycle was 2.6/5, 73% want documentation templates and 142 respondents said they need financial support ([Martel summary](https://martel-innovate.com/?p=363037); [ENISA](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/where-do-smes-stand-in-preparing-for-the-cyber-resilience-act)).
- **Competitors and gap:** Enterprise SCA/SBOM suites (Black Duck, Revenera/Flexera checklist: [Revenera CRA checklist](https://resources.flexera.com/web/eloqua/pdf/Revenera-EU-CRA-Checklist.pdf)), compliance consultancies (advisori, DataGuard: [DataGuard CRA guide](https://www.dataguard.com/cyber-resilience-act/)), trust/compliance platforms adding CRA pages ([Orbiq CRA guide](https://orbiqhq.com/eu-regulations/cyber-resilience-act)). Free open resources include the OpenChain CRA checklist ([OpenChain](https://cra-compliance.openchainproject.org/CRA_Checklist_RC1.html)) and the OpenSSF ebook ([OpenSSF](https://openssf.org/cra-ebook-pdf)). No prices for SMB-tier CRA workflow tools were found. The gap is a self-serve, low-cost "reporting runbook + clock + drafts + SBOM" tool for teams with no PSIRT.
- **Suggested pricing (estimate):** €79/mo per product family (Starter), €199/mo (up to 10 products, SBOM monitoring), and a €990 one-off "CRA readiness pack" covering a templated technical-documentation skeleton and CVD policy.
- **Running cost (estimate):** €30–80/mo (hosting/DB on Supabase/Vercel free-to-pro tiers, LLM API for drafting about €10–30, vulnerability feeds such as OSV/NVD which are free, email).
- **MVP in about 8 weeks:** product/component inventory; SBOM import/generation (Syft/CycloneDX CLI wrapper, GitHub Action); vulnerability matching via OSV with an "actively exploited" flag from the CISA KEV feed; incident/vulnerability intake form plus a public security.txt/CVD-policy generator; a 24h/72h/final-report countdown with LLM-drafted report text in the ENISA SRP field structure; an audit log; user-notification templates.
- **Main risks:** Technical buyers are hard for a non-technical salesperson to sell to. Legal liability if drafts are wrong, so position it as a workflow tool rather than legal advice. Big SCA vendors could add the same features. The SRP field schema may change. The market is concentrated among EU hardware/software makers, so it is not every SMB.
- **2-week no-code validation:** Publish a landing page ("Meet CRA's 24-hour reporting deadline without a PSIRT") plus a free downloadable "CRA reporting runbook" PDF. Post in embedded/IoT communities and LinkedIn. Cold-email 100 small IoT makers found through Kickstarter, Tindie and trade-fair exhibitor lists. Success means 20+ email sign-ups and 5+ calls booked.
- **Interview questions:** (1) Who would file a CRA report at your company if a vulnerability in your product were exploited tomorrow, and how? (2) Do you have an SBOM for each product today, and how do you generate it? (3) How do you currently find out a component you ship is vulnerable or actively exploited? (4) What have you spent or budgeted on CRA readiness (consultants, tools, staff time)? (5) If a tool handled the deadline clock, report drafts and SBOM, what would it need to do for you to pay €100–200/month?

#### Idea 2: "Questionnaire Autopilot + Lite Trust Page" for small B2B vendors (5–100 staff)
- **Customer / buyer:** Small B2B SaaS companies, agencies, IT service firms and MSPs selling to mid-market/enterprise customers or NIS2/DORA-regulated buyers. The buyer is the founder, CTO or head of sales. The user is whoever gets stuck answering questionnaires.
- **Evidence of pain:** Questionnaires become "a second job" ([Wolfia](https://wolfia.com/blog/answer-security-questionnaires-no-security-team)). Even SOC 2 holders still face reviews ([SecurityPal](https://www.securitypalhq.com/blog/5-truths-about-security-questionnaire-automation)). NIS2 Art. 21(2)(d) and DORA registers push questionnaires and contract clauses onto suppliers ([Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/opinion/2026/nis2-turns-supplier-security-into-a-contract-negotiation); [AFM](https://www.afm.nl/en/sector/themas/belangrijke-europese-wet--en-regelgeving/dora/informatieregister)). Third-party involvement in breaches doubled to 30% ([Security Today](https://securitytoday.com/Articles/2025/04/28/Verizons-2025-Data-Breach-Investigations-Report.aspx)).
- **Competitors and gap:** Conveyor (free portal; Pro about $9.6k/yr), SafeBase ($5k–15k+/yr, now Drata), Vanta Trust Center (+about $6k), ShieldPage (from $49/mo), Sprinto (free for ≤20 employees), Wolfia/HeyIris/ComplyJet (AI questionnaire tools) — [G2 Conveyor](https://g2.com/products/conveyor-conveyor/pricing); [Orbiq](https://www.orbiqhq.com/comparisons/safebase-vs-vanta); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/32fce5f1-2a67-4f62-8034-ba3a02bbe8be/); [HeyIris](https://www.heyiris.ai/blog/best-security-questionnaire-software-2026). The gap is a sub-$150/mo tool for vendors *without* SOC 2 that turns honest answers about their stack (Google Workspace/M365, AWS, etc.) into a reusable answer library, NIS2/DORA-mapped answers and a trust page. This is a crowded space.
- **Suggested pricing (estimate):** $49/mo (one trust page plus 5 questionnaires/mo), $149/mo (unlimited, NIS2/DORA/CAIQ mappings, custom domain). Agency/MSP tier $299/mo for 10 clients.
- **Running cost (estimate):** €40–120/mo (LLM costs scale with questionnaire volume; parse Excel/Word with open-source libraries).
- **MVP in about 8 weeks:** onboarding wizard (stack, policies, controls); upload of past questionnaires and policies to build an answer library (RAG); upload of a new Excel/Word questionnaire, AI-draft answers with confidence scores and citations, export in the original format; a public trust page with NDA-gated documents; a library of starter policy templates.
- **Main risks:** Heavy competition, including free tiers (Sprinto, Conveyor). Hallucinated answers create contractual liability, so human review is mandatory. Large GRC players bundle this feature.
- **2-week no-code validation:** The co-founder offers a "we'll fill your next security questionnaire in 48h for €99" concierge service to 50 small SaaS companies and agencies (LinkedIn/cold email), done manually with an LLM plus review. Success means 3+ paid jobs and repeat requests.
- **Interview questions:** (1) How many security/vendor questionnaires did you receive in the last 12 months, and from whom? (2) Who answers them and how many hours does each take? (3) Have you lost or delayed a deal because of a security review or missing SOC 2/ISO? (4) What tools or templates do you use now, and what do you pay? (5) Has any customer cited NIS2, DORA or CRA in a contract or questionnaire to you?

#### Idea 3: "NIS2 Supplier Pack": evidence kit and portal for SMB suppliers of NIS2-regulated companies (and its mirror, a light supplier-assessment tool for mid-sized NIS2 entities)
- **Customer / buyer:** SMB suppliers (IT services, software, logistics, facility and industrial services) to NIS2 "essential/important" entities in the DE, NL, BE, Nordic and CEE markets. The buyer is the owner/MD or ops manager. Mirror customer: mid-sized NIS2 entities (50–250 staff) that must assess dozens of direct suppliers.
- **Evidence of pain:** Art. 21(2)(d) contract flow-down ([nisd2.eu](https://nisd2.eu/en/wiki/law-and-consequences/nis2-supplier-contract-clauses)). 22/27 member states have transposed and four were referred to the CJEU in July 2026 ([Cullen](https://www.cullen-international.com/news/2026/05/How-are-EU-member-states-transposing-NIS2-.html)). About 8,000 Dutch organisations had five weeks to prepare ([ManageEngine](https://www.manageengine.com/insights/it-security/netherlands-gave-8000-organizations-five-weeks-prepare-nis2-shorter-window-compliance-constraint)). Implementing Reg 2024/2690 sets 150+ controls for MSPs and digital providers ([Bastion](https://bastion.tech/learn/nis2/supply-chain-security)). 61% of MSP customers expect regulatory help ([CyberSmart](https://cybersmart.co.uk/msp-cybersecurity-report-2026/)).
- **Competitors and gap:** Full GRC platforms (Vanta/Drata/Secureframe; Sprinto; Orbiq vendor assurance: [Orbiq](https://www.orbiqhq.com/eu-regulations/vendor-assurance-nis2)), supplier-assurance platforms (e.g. Aprovall), consultancies, and the ISMS Copilot AI assistant ([ISMS Copilot](https://ismscopilot.hc.ferndesk.com/how-to-manage-nis2-supply-chain-security-using-ai-pmqz9)). The gap is a cheap, NIS2-specific, supplier-side "answer my customer once" pack with country-specific mappings. No SMB price points for NIS2-specific supplier tools were found.
- **Suggested pricing (estimate):** €49/mo supplier plan; €199/mo buyer plan (assess up to 50 suppliers); one-off €299 "NIS2 supplier readiness report".
- **Running cost (estimate):** €30–80/mo.
- **MVP in about 8 weeks:** a 40–60 question NIS2 Art. 21 self-assessment mapped to 2024/2690 controls and ISO 27001; auto-generated policies (incident response, access control, backup, supplier management); an evidence upload checklist; a shareable "supplier security profile" link/PDF; a buyer-side mode that sends the same questionnaire to suppliers and scores responses.
- **Main risks:** Country-by-country variance; overlap with Idea 2 (they could merge); buyers may demand ISO 27001 anyway; selling into DACH may need German, so English-only limits reach.
- **2-week no-code validation:** A Typeform "NIS2 supplier readiness score" plus a manual PDF report, promoted to SMB IT firms in NL, BE, DE, SE and Nordic LinkedIn groups. Interview 10 suppliers who have received NIS2 clauses. Success means 30+ assessments completed and 5 people willing to pay €299 for the report.
- **Interview questions:** (1) Has a customer sent you NIS2-related contract clauses or questionnaires? Can you share one? (2) What did they ask you to prove, and how long did it take? (3) Did you have to buy anything (consultant, ISO cert, tool) to keep the customer? (4) How many of your customers are likely in NIS2 scope? (5) Would a reusable "supplier security profile" you send to all customers save you time, and what would it be worth per month?

#### Idea 4: "InsureReady": cyber-insurance readiness, evidence and questionnaire autopilot (SMB direct plus MSP/broker channel)
- **Customer / buyer:** US and UK SMBs (10–250 staff) renewing or applying for cyber insurance; MSPs that prepare renewals for clients; small brokers. The buyer is the owner, office manager/CFO, or MSP account manager. These are non-technical buyers, which suits a non-technical salesperson.

UK Cyber Essentials v3.3 (Apr 2026) tightened MFA, and SMEs get a 75% subsidy ([Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/security/smes-offered-75-percent-discount-cyber-essentials)).
- **Competitors and gap:** Insurer-provided tools (e.g. Coalition's own scanning/Control platform), MSP checklists, compliance platforms. No standalone SMB-priced "insurance evidence pack" tool with public pricing was found (gap in research). - **Suggested pricing (estimate):** SMB $39–79/mo or a $299 one-off "renewal pack"; MSP $8–15 per client per month, white-label.
- **Running cost (estimate):** €20–60/mo (Graph and Google Admin APIs are free; external DNS/email checks are cheap).
- **MVP in about 8 weeks:** OAuth connect to M365/Google Workspace (read-only) for an MFA coverage report, admins without MFA, legacy auth, external forwarding rules and stale accounts; external checks (SPF/DKIM/DMARC, exposed RDP/VPN banners, TLS); a 30-question canonical insurance questionnaire with auto-filled answers and evidence screenshots/exports; a PDF "evidence pack" for the broker; a renewal reminder.
- **Main risks:** Carriers each use different forms; liability if an attested answer is wrong; insurers may bundle free scanning; Microsoft Graph permission consent is a trust hurdle for an unknown vendor.
- **2-week no-code validation:** Partner with 2–3 MSPs or small brokers. Hand-produce 5 evidence packs from free tools (M365 admin exports, MXToolbox, Shodan) for their clients' renewals. Ask whether they would pay $10/client/month for this to be automatic. Success means 2 MSPs say yes with named clients.
- **Interview questions:** (1) Walk me through your last cyber-insurance application or renewal. Who filled it in and how long did it take? (2) Which questions were hardest to answer truthfully or prove? (3) Has a carrier raised your premium, excluded coverage or declined you because of a control gap? (4) What evidence did the broker or carrier ask for? (5) Who would pay for a tool that produces the evidence pack: you, your MSP or your broker, and how much per year?

#### Idea 5: "PostureReport for MSPs": white-label external attack surface plus email-security monitor and prospecting reports

- **Interview questions:** (1) How do you currently show clients their external risk, and how often? (2) Which scanning or reporting tools do you pay for and what do they cost per client? (3) Do you use risk reports for prospecting, and do they convert? (4) What makes a report usable for a non-technical business owner? (5) What price per domain or client would make this a no-brainer?

- **Customer / buyer:** Companies with 5–25 staff priced out by 25-seat minimums; MSPs in non-English EU markets. The buyer is the owner or MSP.
- **Evidence of pain:** BEC claims up about 30% in H2 2025 ([Risk & Insurance](https://riskandinsurance.com/business-email-compromise-claims-jump-30-as-a-i-reshapes-cyber-risk/)); AI phishing kits make micro businesses targets ([Insurance Business UK](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/cyber/ai-phishing-kits-are-making-micro-businesses-worth-attacking-coalition-warns-593811.aspx)); KnowBe4 has a 25-seat minimum ([CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/software/soc2-compliance/knowbe4/)).
- **Competitors and gap:** KnowBe4 ($1.63–3.75/user/mo), Usecure (no minimums), Riot ($6.89/user), Hoxhunt, Microsoft Attack Simulation (E5) — [hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/labs/switch/knowbe4). The remaining gap is narrow: localisation and ultra-simple setup.
- **Suggested pricing (estimate):** $2–3/user/mo, no minimum; MSP bundles.
- **Running cost (estimate):** €30–80/mo (email sending reputation and deliverability work is the hidden cost).
- **MVP in about 8 weeks:** LLM-generated, localised phishing templates; campaign scheduler; tracking landing pages; 2-minute training on click; monthly report.
- **Main risks:** Crowded; email deliverability and allow-listing friction; abuse (the platform could be misused for real phishing); low differentiation.
- **2-week no-code validation:** Run 3 manual phishing tests (with consent) for small firms in Moldova/Romania/Sweden using GoPhish, then ask for a paid follow-up.
- **Interview questions:** (1) Has anyone on your team fallen for a phishing or fake-invoice email? (2) Do you do any security training now, and why or why not? (3) Would you pay for a monthly test plus 2-minute training, and how much per person? (4) Does your insurer or customer require training evidence? (5) What language does your team prefer for training?

#### Deprioritised (evidence-based reasons)
- **PCI DSS e-commerce script monitoring for small merchants:** SAQ A merchants no longer need 6.4.3/11.6.1 since Mar 2025 ([Adyen](https://docs.adyen.com/pt/development-resources/pci-dss-compliance-guide/saq-a-eligibility)), which removes most small-merchant demand; a crowded vendor field (cside, Feroot, Report URI, HUMAN).
- **HIPAA Security Rule modernisation tool:** The final rule is delayed to at least July 2027 ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/feds-push-back-hipaa-security-rule-overhaul-july-2027)).
- **AI Act high-risk compliance:** Delayed to Dec 2027/Aug 2028; few SMBs build high-risk systems ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)).
- **Pure DMARC monitoring:** Commoditised at free–$36/mo ([TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/compare-products/easydmarc-vs-valimail)); bootstrapped peers report about $1k MRR ([Indie Hackers Unspam](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/unspam)).
- **DORA register tool:** Buyers are financial entities with long procurement cycles, and the first registers were already filed in March 2026 ([BaFin deadline via advisori](https://www.advisori.de/en/blog/dora-information-register-2026-bafin-reporting-deadline-is-running-what-financial-companies-have)). Better folded into Idea 2 as "DORA-ready answers".

### Inferences
- The €800/3-month budget is enough for any of Ideas 1–7 if they are built on free/low tiers (Supabase/Vercel/Cloudflare), with LLM costs kept to about €10–50/mo during the MVP. The bigger constraints are 15 h/week and trust/verification hurdles: Google restricted scopes, Microsoft Graph consent, Chrome Web Store.
- Ideas 2 and 3 can merge into one product ("answer your customers' security, NIS2 and DORA questions"). Ideas 4 and 5 can share a scanning engine, with insurance readiness as the SMB-facing story and the posture report as the MSP-facing story.
- For a non-technical Swedish salesperson selling in English, the easiest buyers are owners and office managers (Idea 4) and MSP owners (Ideas 4 and 5). CRA (Idea 1) needs a technical conversation, though the founder developer can join calls.

### Gaps
- No SMB pricing found for competitors in Ideas 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7. This is the largest open question for positioning.
- Validation-success thresholds and pricing are the researcher's judgement, not sourced benchmarks.
- Google Workspace restricted-scope verification costs and timelines were not researched.
